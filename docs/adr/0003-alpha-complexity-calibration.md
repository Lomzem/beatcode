# Calibrate alpha problems with all observed timing evidence

The private alpha ranks the earliest server-admitted submission ultimately accepted; runtime is a complexity gate. After the fixed max/min spread screen rejected most original problems, independent primary-source review found that eight short wall-time repeats do not establish host reproducibility. We accept the additional fairness risk of removing that rejection screen under the named decision policy `alpha-complexity-all-history-v1`, while keeping observed spread visible. G6 and historical spread failures remain failed; this is a changed decision policy, not a passed old gate.

The policy uses all genuine observations under identical immutable measurement semantics and bindings, including initial and partial/full retry history. Every accepted reference needs at least eight complete AC repeats; every observed accepted attempt must satisfy the unchanged per-test, 7500ms aggregate and 15000ms attempt bounds. The public limit remains roundUp100(max(500, 2.5 × maximum accepted per-test wall)), capped at 3000ms; every slow reference needs at least eight genuine measurements whose all-history minimum is at least 2.5 × that limit. Validator, slow-sample correctness, WA, source/test/environment binding, cleanup ownership and explicit one-shot empty-ERROR recovery remain required. Outliers and genuine failures cannot be discarded or replaced.

Each re-evaluation appends its named decision and evidence provenance without erasing prior decisions or receipts. Measurement changes require fresh calibration treatment. Same-semantics receipts can be re-evaluated, with no claim of prospective validation. Production recalibration and passive near-threshold withdrawal remain release requirements. The alternative is a more reproducible judge host; CPU-heavy content alone cannot guarantee it. No statistical confidence bound or stable verdict near the time limit is promised.

## Passive monitoring amendment (2026-10-23)

Monitoring examines the last 20 completed scored verdicts. A non-TLE verdict with stored per-test measurements is near-threshold when its slowest test lies within [limit / 1.5, limit × 1.5]. TLE measurements are censored by an execution limit and cannot establish proximity to that limit, so they are excluded from measurements and the near-threshold numerator. WA and other non-TLE verdicts with measurements remain eligible.

The denominator remains all scored verdicts in that fixed window, including TLE and verdicts without measurements. After 20 scored verdicts, a rate strictly above 5% withdraws the version to `calibrating`. Using only the eligible measurement count would instead let one measured verdict trigger withdrawal when the other 19 were censored; this amendment retains the existing scored-window policy. This passive safeguard is a heuristic, not a new calibration decision policy or statistical guarantee. It does not alter historical calibration hashes or qualification evidence.
